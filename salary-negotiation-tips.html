Salary Negotiation Tips: How to Get Paid What You're Worth

📅 September 25, 2026 | ⏱️ 11 min read | ✍️ ResumeRocket Team

Most professionals accept their first job offer without negotiating. This is a costly mistake.

Studies show that 84% of employers expect candidates to negotiate — yet only 37% actually do. More importantly, not negotiating your first salary can cost you over $500,000 across a 40-year career, because every raise is based on your starting number.

The good news: salary negotiation is a learnable skill. With the right research, timing, and scripts, you can confidently ask for more — and often get it.

Negotiating your salary is expected — not rude

1. When to Negotiate (Timing is Everything)

The Wrong Times:

  • ❌ During the first interview.
  • ❌ Before you have an offer in hand.
  • ❌ When the employer asks "What are your salary expectations?"

The Right Time:

  • ✅ After you receive a formal written offer.
  • ✅ When they've already invested in you (multiple interviews).
  • ✅ When they've told you they want you.

2. How to Handle "What Are Your Salary Expectations?"

This question is designed to box you in. Here are three safe responses:

Response 1: Deflect Gently

"I'd prefer to learn more about the role and responsibilities before discussing compensation. Could you share the budgeted range for this position?"

Response 2: Give a Wide Range

"Based on my research and experience, I'm considering roles in the $X to $Y range. But I'm flexible depending on the full package. What range has been budgeted?"

Response 3: Use Their Own Data

"I'd love to align with your internal compensation structure. Could you share what range this role typically falls into?"

Never give a single number first — it anchors the conversation lower.

3. Research Your Market Value

Before negotiating, know your numbers. Use these tools:

  • Glassdoor — Company-specific salary data.
  • Levels.fyi — For tech roles, breakdown by level and company.
  • Payscale — Industry-specific benchmarks.
  • LinkedIn Salary — Compare by title and region.
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics — Official market data.

Find three data points. Use the 75th percentile as your target (not the median).

Data-backed negotiation is 3x more likely to succeed

4. The Negotiation Script That Works

Opening the Conversation:

"Thank you so much for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about this role. I've reviewed the details and would like to discuss the base salary. Based on my research and experience in [specific skill], I was hoping we could explore something closer to $X."

If They Say "This is our best offer":

"I understand budgets are tight. If the base salary is fixed, could we explore signing bonuses, additional PTO, a 6-month performance review, or remote flexibility?"

If They Ask for Justification:

"Three factors: my [X years] of experience in [specific skill], the achievement of [specific result at previous role], and the market data showing roles like this at $X–$Y in our region."

5. Negotiate the Full Package, Not Just Salary

If base salary is locked, negotiate these instead:

  • Signing bonus — Often easier to approve than base increase.
  • Annual bonus % — Higher upside without affecting base budget.
  • Equity or stock — Particularly in startups.
  • Extra PTO — 5 extra days = 2.5% effective raise.
  • Remote work flexibility — Saves commute costs and time.
  • Professional development budget — Courses, conferences, certifications.
  • Earlier performance review — 6-month review instead of 12.

The best negotiators discuss the full package, not just base pay

6. What NOT to Do During Negotiation

  • ❌ Don't make it personal: "I need more because I have bills."
  • ❌ Don't threaten: "I have other offers" (unless you actually do).
  • ❌ Don't accept instantly: "Let me think it over" is always acceptable.
  • ❌ Don't negotiate over text: Use email or phone.
  • ❌ Don't reveal your current salary: Focus on market value.

7. How to Accept a Good Counter Offer

When they meet your number, respond professionally:

"Thank you — I appreciate you working with me on this. I'm thrilled to accept the offer and I'm looking forward to starting on [date]."

Then get it in writing before signing anything.

8. What If They Say No?

Sometimes companies genuinely can't budge. If that happens:

  • Ask for a 6-month performance review with a guaranteed raise tied to specific metrics.
  • Ask for a signing bonus (one-time cost, easier to approve).
  • Accept gracefully — but note the data for your next review.

Even an unsuccessful negotiation often primes your manager to think about your compensation more seriously in the future.

Quick Negotiation Checklist

  • ☐ Research market value (3+ data points)
  • ☐ Wait until you have a written offer
  • ☐ Never give a single number first
  • ☐ Anchor with the 75th percentile
  • ☐ Negotiate the full package
  • ☐ Stay professional and collaborative
  • ☐ Get final agreement in writing

Final Thoughts

Negotiating your salary isn't about being aggressive — it's about being prepared, professional, and confident in your value.

The worst outcome is "no" — and even then, you've planted a seed for future raises. The best outcome is thousands of extra dollars per year, compounding across your entire career.

You're worth more than you're being paid. Ask for it.
